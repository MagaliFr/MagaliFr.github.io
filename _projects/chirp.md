During my PhD, I was part of the **CHIRP project**, which investigated how human pressures affect bird populations across different stages of their life cycle and how these effects accumulate.

Using the Eurasian oystercatcher as a model species, my PhD focused on the **reproductive ecology of inland-breeding oystercatchers**. I studied drivers of reduced reproductive success in breeding areas and investigated potential carry-over effects of winter body condition on subsequent reproductive performance.

The work combined long-term ecological monitoring, citizen-science data and quantitative approaches including structural equation modelling, Bayesian methods and mark-recapture models.
