This project originated from my MSc thesis in **Forest and Nature Conservation at Wageningen University**, carried out in collaboration with the **Institute for Terrestrial and Aquatic Wildlife Research, University of Veterinary Medicine Hannover**.

I investigated how **environmental and physiological factors influence reproduction in wild boar (*Sus scrofa*)** in an agricultural landscape in northern Germany. The work used long-term reproductive data to examine variation in litter size, timing of birth and foetal sex ratios.

One part of the project showed that maternal body weight, oak mast availability and specific weather conditions were associated with variation in litter size. The project resulted in a peer-reviewed publication:

**Frauendorf, M., Gethöffer, F., Siebert, U. & Keuling, O. (2016).** The influence of environmental and physiological factors on the litter size of wild boar (*Sus scrofa*) in an agriculture dominated area in Germany. *Science of the Total Environment*, **541**, 877–882.  
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scitotenv.2015.09.128

This work shaped my continuing interest in **ungulate ecology, reproduction, environmental drivers and wildlife management**, themes that also connect to my current research.
